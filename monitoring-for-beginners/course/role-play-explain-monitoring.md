# Role Play: Explain Monitoring to a Shop Owner

**Monitoring and Observability for Beginners · Coraxshift DevOps Academy** · ⏱️ 15 minutes (after the assignment)

## 🎭 The scenario

A shop owner runs a small online shop. Last week the website was down for six hours overnight, and she only found out when a customer called her in the morning. Her nephew says she needs "monitoring". She asks: *"What is that, will it cost me a lot, and will it wake me up for nothing?"*

She is sharp, but she is not technical. She will stop you at any jargon.

## 🎯 Your job

Explain **what monitoring is** in **about one minute**, in plain words, with **no unexplained jargon**. Answer her three questions:

1. What is monitoring?
2. What will it cost me?
3. Will it wake me up for nothing?

## 💡 Ideas to build on

- Monitoring is **looking at the till, the shelves and the door on purpose**, all day, and ringing a bell when something is off.
- Two parts: **numbers over time** (how many customers, how many errors, how slow) and **a diary** (what happened at 10:03).
- The tools can be **free and open-source**, and cost mostly **time to set up well**.
- Waking her for nothing is a real risk. A good alert only rings for **things customers feel**, and says **what to check first**.
- The goal is not pretty charts. It is to **hear about trouble before the customer does**.

> 📖 **From my café:** In my cybercafe I ran 12 or more computers. HandyCafe showed me which sessions were running, and I also walked the floor. When a machine froze I knew within minutes, not when the customer was already angry. This is the same idea for a website.

## 🧪 A starting script (say it in your own words)

> "Right now you find out the shop is broken when a customer tells you. Monitoring is like having a person who watches the till and the door all day and rings a bell when something is wrong. It keeps numbers, like how many orders and how many failed, and it keeps a diary of what happened. Most of the tools are free, so the cost is mostly the time to set it up properly. We make sure the bell only rings for things that matter, like orders failing, and each alert says what to check first, so you are not woken for nothing."

Do **not** read it out. Say it in your own words.

## ✅ Pass check

- [ ] You spoke for about one minute
- [ ] You answered all three of her questions
- [ ] You did not use "metric", "Prometheus", "alert rule" or "latency" without explaining it
- [ ] You used one everyday comparison
- [ ] You were honest that false alarms are a risk, and said how you reduce them
- [ ] The listener could repeat your explanation in her own words
