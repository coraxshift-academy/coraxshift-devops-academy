# Role Play: Explain CI/CD to a Business Owner

**CI/CD for Beginners · Coraxshift DevOps Academy** · ⏱️ 15 minutes (after the assignment)

## 🎭 The scenario

A business owner has a booking website. Every time her developer changes something, it takes days, someone has to do it by hand late at night, and last month a small change took the site down for an hour. The developer says, "We need a CI/CD pipeline." She asks you:

> *"What is a pipeline, really? Will it stop my site breaking when you make changes? And does it mean nobody checks anything any more?"*

She is sharp but not technical. She will stop you at any jargon.

## 🎯 Your job

Explain CI/CD in **about one minute**, in plain words, with **no unexplained jargon**. Answer her questions:

1. What is a pipeline, in a way I can picture?
2. Will it stop my site breaking, and does it mean nobody checks?

## 💡 Ideas to build on

- Every change goes through **the same steps, in the same order, automatically**, like a ticket desk where every customer follows the same process.
- The steps include **checks**: does it still work? If any check fails, the change **stops** and the team is told straight away, while it is small.
- Passing changes are **packaged and delivered** the same way every time, so it does not depend on one tired person at night.
- For the important step, a **person still approves**. Automation does the boring checking, and people keep the decisions.
- If something does go wrong, going **back** is quick, because the same process can deliver the previous version.
- It is not magic: it can only catch what someone **wrote a check for**. It reduces breakages, it does not remove them.

> 📖 **From my café:** In my café, every customer followed the same steps, and a gate at each step stopped problems early. When I noticed anything unusual, I investigated before it hurt the business. A pipeline gives a software team that same discipline.

## 🧪 A starting script (say it in your own words)

> "Think of a ticket desk where every customer goes through the same steps, and a gate stops anything that is wrong. A pipeline does that for changes to your website. Every change is checked automatically, the same way, every time, and if a check fails the change stops straight away, while it is small. If it passes, it is delivered in the same careful way, and for the final step to your real site, a person approves. If something still goes wrong, we can go back quickly. It will not make mistakes impossible, because it can only check what we have taught it to check, but it makes them smaller, rarer and quicker to undo."

Do **not** read it out. Say it in your own words.

## ✅ Pass check

- [ ] You spoke for about one minute
- [ ] You answered both of her questions
- [ ] You said honestly what a pipeline cannot do
- [ ] You mentioned that a person still approves
- [ ] You used one everyday comparison
- [ ] You did not use "workflow", "artifact" or "environment" without explaining them
- [ ] The listener could repeat your explanation in her own words
