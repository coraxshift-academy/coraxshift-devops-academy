# Internet & Tech Essentials

**Coraxshift DevOps Academy · Learn. Build. Deploy. Grow.**
*A complete course on its own. No other course needed.*

## 👋 Welcome

If you have never worked in tech, you are in exactly the right place.

Everything you do online, from sending a WhatsApp message to paying for something on your phone, uses the same few ideas. Once you understand them, tech stops feeling like magic and starts feeling like plumbing: pipes, addresses and a few simple rules. This course shows you the plumbing.

> 📖 **From my café:** I opened my first business in August 2007: a cybercafé with more than twelve computers. Customers bought browsing time on a ticket for 30 minutes, an hour, two hours or a full day, and typed it in at their seat to start. Running it meant understanding how the computers connected, how customers reached the internet, and how the whole setup worked. Much of what I knew came from watching technicians and trying things myself. That taught me something important: using technology is not the same as understanding how it works. This course gives you the basics behind the screen.

Take your time. Every step tells you what to type, what it does, what you should see, and what to do if yours looks different. Getting an error is not failing. It is how every engineer learns.

## Who this course is for

- Complete beginners who have never used a terminal
- Career changers moving into DevOps, cloud or software
- Anyone who uses the internet every day and wants to know how it actually works

## What you will be able to do by the end

1. Explain what happens when you type `google.com` and press Enter
2. Explain packets, IP addresses, TCP/IP, HTTP and HTTPS in your own words
3. Use `ping` and `nslookup` and read what they tell you
4. Explain DNS and choose the right DNS record for a job
5. Compare a two-tier app with a three-tier app and draw both
6. Set up VS Code, open its terminal and run your first commands
7. Ask an AI assistant good questions and check its answers
8. Fix problems with a 5-step method and ask for help clearly
9. Explain all of this to someone who has never heard these words

You need:

## How the course works

| Part | What it is | Why it helps |
|---|---|---|
| Classes | Step-by-step guided practice | You learn by doing |
| Assignment | Six tasks, in your own words | Proves you understood |
| Role play | Explain DNS to a shop owner | If you can explain it simply, you know it |
| Quiz | 12 questions | Shows what to revisit |

Every class follows the same five parts for each step, so you always know what is coming:

1. **Why this matters**: one sentence and an everyday comparison
2. **Run it**: what to type
3. **What it does**
4. **What you should see**
5. **If yours looks different**: the most likely reason and the fix

> 💡 **Your output will never match ours character for character.** Numbers, names and times change from computer to computer. Compare the *meaning*, not every letter.

## Safety rules

1. **Never paste passwords, tokens or keys** into screenshots, AI tools or anything public.
2. **Only run the commands in the lessons.** If you are unsure what something does, ask first.
3. **Do not change router or network settings** unless you own them and know why.
4. **Blur your username** and other personal details before sharing a screenshot.
5. **Save your work in your own portfolio repository**, not in this course repository.

## Pass check

You are ready to move on when you can:

- Open a terminal on your own computer
- Run `ping` and `nslookup` and say what the answer means
- Explain DNS in plain words
- Open VS Code, open the terminal inside it and run `whoami`
- Show your assignment folder in your portfolio
- Score 11 out of 12 or more in the quiz
