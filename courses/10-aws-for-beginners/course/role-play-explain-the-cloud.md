# Role Play: Explain the Cloud to a Shop Owner

**AWS for Beginners · Coraxshift DevOps Academy** · ⏱️ 15 minutes (after the assignment)

## 🎭 The scenario

A shop owner keeps her customer list and stock records on one computer in the back room. Last month it broke and she lost three days of work. Her nephew says she should "move to the cloud". She is worried: *"Is it safe? Will I get a surprise bill? And what happens if I stop paying?"*

She is sharp, but she is not technical. She will stop you at any jargon.

## 🎯 Your job

Explain **what the cloud is** in **about one minute**, in plain words, with **no unexplained jargon**. Make sure you answer her three questions:

1. Is it safe?
2. How do I avoid a surprise bill?
3. What if I want to leave?

## 💡 Ideas to build on

- The cloud is **renting** computers by the hour in someone else's well-guarded building, instead of buying and keeping one in the back room.
- It is safer from a broken computer, because the same information is kept in more than one place. It is only as safe as **who has the keys**, so locks, a second lock (MFA) and who may enter matter.
- A bill is a **meter**, like electricity. You can set an alert that emails you before it gets high. It warns you, so someone still has to switch things off.
- You pay for what is **running**. Switching off what you do not use is the main skill.
- You can copy your records out and leave. Plan this before you start.

> 📖 **From my café:** In my cybercafe I ran 12 or more computers, and customers paid by the hour using browsing-time tickets in a system called HandyCafe. Everyone understood paying for time used. The cloud works the same way: you pay for the time something is switched on, and the monitor tells you who is using what.

## 🧪 A starting script (say it in your own words)

> "Right now your records live on one computer in the back room. If it breaks, they are gone. The cloud means renting space in a very well guarded building, where your records are kept in more than one place, so one broken machine does not lose them. You pay like electricity, only for the time you use, and we set an alert so you hear about it before the bill is big. You can also take all your records out whenever you want. The part that needs care is who holds the keys, so we add a second lock, a code on the phone, before anyone gets in."

Do **not** read it out. Say it in your own words.

## ✅ Pass check

- [ ] You spoke for about one minute
- [ ] You answered all three of her questions
- [ ] You did not use "instance", "region", "bucket" or "subscription" without explaining them
- [ ] You used one everyday comparison
- [ ] You were honest that the bill alert **warns** and does not stop spending
- [ ] The listener could repeat your explanation in her own words
