# Role Play: Explain Automation to a Shop Owner

**Bash Scripting for Beginners · Coraxshift DevOps Academy** · ⏱️ 15 minutes (after the assignment)

## 🎭 The scenario

A shop owner runs a busy bookshop. Every evening her assistant counts the sales, copies the day's records to a memory stick, and checks that the website is still open. It takes about 40 minutes, and last month the assistant forgot to copy the records the very day the shop computer failed.

She asks you:

> *"What is a script? Why would a program do this better than my assistant? And how do I know the copy is really good?"*

She is clever, but she is not technical. She will stop you if you use a word without explaining it.

## 🎯 Your job

Explain automation in **about one minute**, in plain words, with **no unexplained jargon**. Make sure you answer her three questions:

1. What is a script?
2. Why is it better than doing the job by hand?
3. How do we know the backup really works?

## 💡 Ideas to build on

- A **script** is a written recipe the computer follows exactly, the same way, every time.
- A person forgets, gets tired and makes slips. A script does not, and it can run at 2 a.m.
- A copy you never tested is only a hope. The **restore test** takes the copy back out and compares it with the original. That is how you *know*.

> 📖 **From my café:** This is how I would put it: "Imagine walking round twelve computers to check each one, every day. Now imagine writing those checks down once and letting the computer run them for you. That is automation: you write the routine, and the machine does it the same way every time."

## 🧪 A starting script (say it in your own words)

> "A script is a recipe that a computer follows without getting tired. Your assistant does the same three jobs every evening. If we write the recipe once, the computer can do it in a few seconds, at the same time every day, and it never forgets. And we don't just trust the copy. Every time, the computer takes the copy back out and checks it against the original, so you know it works before you ever need it. If something is wrong, it tells you straight away, instead of you finding out on the worst day."

Do **not** read it out. Say it in your own words. If you read it out, it will not help you.

## ✅ Pass check

- [ ] You spoke for about one minute
- [ ] You answered all three of her questions
- [ ] You did not use "script", "backup", "restore" or "automation" without explaining it
- [ ] You used one everyday comparison
- [ ] The listener could repeat your explanation in her own words
