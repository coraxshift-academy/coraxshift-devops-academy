# Role Play: Explain Delivery to a Business Owner

**Software Delivery Lifecycle for Beginners · Coraxshift DevOps Academy** · ⏱️ 15 minutes (after the assignment)

## 🎭 The scenario

A restaurant owner is paying a developer to build an ordering app. Last month the developer "finished" it, the owner launched it on a Friday evening, and the app showed the wrong prices for two hours. Customers were angry, and nobody knew how to undo it quickly.

She asks you:

> *"Why do you keep asking me to write down what 'done' means? Why do you test everything before it goes live? And what is this 'rollback' thing you keep talking about?"*

She is clever, but she is not technical. She will stop you if you use a word without explaining it.

## 🎯 Your job

Explain safe delivery in **about one minute**, in plain words, with **no unexplained jargon**. Make sure you answer her three questions:

1. Why write down what "done" means?
2. Why test before going live?
3. What is a rollback?

## 💡 Ideas to build on

- Writing down "done" is like agreeing the cake order **before** baking: size, flavour, pickup time. Nobody argues later.
- Testing is **tasting before you serve**. You would rather find the salt problem in the kitchen than at the customer's table.
- A rollback is **the spare key**. If a new change goes wrong, you put the previous working version back quickly, and fix the problem calmly afterwards.
- Small changes are easier to check and easier to undo.

> 📖 **From my café:** This is how I would put it: "Imagine changing something on all twelve café computers at once and getting it wrong. Everything stops. The safe way is to try the change on one machine, watch it, and only then do the rest, with a way back if it goes badly. Delivering software safely works the same way: small steps, checked, with a plan to go back."

## 🧪 A starting script (say it in your own words)

> "Think of ordering a cake. Before it is baked, we write down the size, the flavour and the pickup time, so everyone agrees what 'done' looks like. Then, before the cake leaves the kitchen, we taste it and check the writing, because it is much cheaper to find a mistake in the kitchen than at the party. And if something still goes wrong after delivery, we keep a spare: the last version we know was good. Putting that back quickly is what we call a rollback. It is not about blame. It is about getting your customers back to normal fast, then fixing the cause calmly."

Do **not** read it out. Say it in your own words. If you read it out, it will not help you.

## ✅ Pass check

- [ ] You spoke for about one minute
- [ ] You answered all three of her questions
- [ ] You did not use "acceptance criteria", "testing", "release" or "rollback" without explaining them
- [ ] You used one everyday comparison
- [ ] The listener could repeat your explanation in her own words
